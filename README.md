## Is the OKX Card a real credit card?

No. This is the first thing to clear up because the wording around crypto cards can get unnecessarily slippery.

A conventional credit card lets you borrow against a credit limit. You repay later, and interest may apply if you carry a balance. The OKX Card works differently: you need supported funds in your OKX Pay account, and purchases are funded from that balance.

OKX’s card materials describe the product as a payment card connected to OKX Pay. In the EEA, the supported payment currencies listed by OKX are USDC and USDG. The card converts the balance at the time of purchase, while the merchant receives a normal fiat payment through the card network.

That means:

| Feature | OKX Card | Traditional credit card |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Borrowing facility | No | Yes |
| Credit limit | No | Yes |
| Interest charges | No credit interest, but crypto conversion costs may apply | May apply |
| Funding source | Supported OKX Pay assets | Credit line from card issuer |
| Card type | Virtual crypto-funded payment card | Credit card |
| Payment network | Mastercard or Visa, depending on region | Mastercard, Visa, Amex, or another network |
| Cashback | May be available on eligible transactions | Depends on issuer and card program |
| KYC | Required | Required by issuer |
| Physical card | Not currently issued according to OKX’s general card help page | Usually available, depending on issuer |

So if your goal is to borrow money, build a traditional credit history, or pay for a purchase next month, the OKX Card is the wrong product. If your goal is to spend stablecoins or another supported balance through a familiar card network, it may fit better.

## How the OKX Card works

The payment flow is designed to hide most of the crypto mechanics from the merchant.

A typical transaction looks like this:

1. You hold a supported asset in OKX Pay.
2. You use the virtual OKX Card online, in an app, or through a supported mobile wallet.
3. OKX converts the eligible balance into the transaction currency.
4. The merchant receives a standard card payment.
5. The crypto balance is reduced by the amount needed for the purchase and any applicable conversion cost.

For the EEA version, OKX says USDC and USDG remain in the user’s wallet until the moment of purchase. The conversion then happens when the card transaction is processed. OKX lists Mastercard as the network for that product and says the card can be used at merchants that accept Mastercard.

The precise supported assets and card network can vary by jurisdiction. OKX’s broader card help page says the card may be Visa or Mastercard depending on the region, and that eligibility must be checked inside the OKX app.

This regional structure matters. Crypto products are rarely one global product with one global set of terms. The same brand can offer different card issuers, supported currencies, cashback rules, and fee schedules in different countries.

For example, on a €100 purchase, a 0.1% spread would be approximately €0.10 before considering any other exchange-rate effects or merchant-side costs. The actual amount can depend on the transaction route and the applicable regional terms.

Do not treat “no annual fee” as “every transaction is free.” Check:

- Which asset funds the transaction
- Whether the purchase is converted into another currency
- Whether the card program applies a market spread
- Whether the purchase falls into an excluded merchant category
- Whether the transaction is reversed, refunded, or left unsettled

## What are the main limitations?

The OKX Card is most useful when you already hold supported assets and understand how crypto conversion works. It is less suitable if you are looking for a conventional bank card.

The main limitations are:

- It is not a credit card.
- It does not provide a borrowing limit.
- Availability depends on country and account eligibility.
- The card is virtual only according to OKX’s general card help page.
- Supported payment currencies vary by region.
- Cashback may require USDG specifically.
- Cashback can have monthly caps and excluded merchant categories.
- Stablecoin conversion may include a market spread even when the card itself has no transaction fee.
- Crypto balances and stablecoins carry financial and operational risks.
- A payment can be declined when the eligible OKX Pay balance is insufficient.

The reward cap also changes the economics for larger spenders. A 10% headline rate sounds substantial, but it is only useful up to the applicable monthly ceiling. Once the cap is reached, additional purchases may earn no further cashback for that period.

## OKX Card versus a traditional credit card

Choose the OKX Card when:

- You already use OKX Pay.
- You want to spend supported stablecoins through a card network.
- You prefer a virtual card.
- You understand conversion spreads and regional restrictions.
- Cashback is available in your jurisdiction and matches your preferred payment asset.

A traditional credit card is usually more appropriate when:

- You need a credit limit.
- You want to borrow and repay later.
- You want traditional credit-history reporting.
- You need purchase protections tied to a bank card.
- You want a physical card with broad domestic support.
- You do not want your payment balance linked to crypto assets.

The product names may sound similar in search results, but the financial mechanics are very different. The OKX Card is a way to spend an eligible OKX balance. It is not a crypto version of a bank-issued credit line.
